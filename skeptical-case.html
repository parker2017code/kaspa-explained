What could go wrong

Kaspa risks and open questions

The case for Kaspa rests on a handful of separate claims, and any one of them can fail without the others failing. Here is what would break each one.

Seven risks, and what would settle each one

Verification cost

Higher block rates raise the bandwidth, CPU, and storage a node needs. Push that too far and only a few well-funded operators can still check the chain.

What would settle it

Many separate operators running nodes, APIs, explorers, and indexers, not just fast blocks sounding good.

Security budget

Block rewards decline over time. Unless fees and demand replace that revenue, miner income falls and so does the cost of attacking the network.

What would settle it, and what pays for the hash rate today

Fees, demand, and value paying for enough hash power over time, not price or enthusiasm alone.

Kaspa's block reward is 2.31246515 KAS (api.kaspa.org/info/blockreward): 864,000 blocks a day at 10 blocks per second, 1,997,970 KAS of new supply daily. At $0.02934513 per KAS (CoinGecko, fetched live), that's about $58,631 a day paid to miners, funding a network running at roughly 311.5 PH/s (api.kaspa.org/info/hashrate). Fees are a rounding error next to that: about 77,207 transactions a day at a median $0.000063 (this site's own chain data) comes to $4.86 a day, roughly 1/12,054th of the subsidy, 0.0083%. The other 99.99% is new supply, not demand.

The subsidy is also shrinking on its own schedule. Kaspa's reward steps down every month by a factor of 2−1/12, about 5.61%, per rusty-kaspa's coinbase.rs subsidy table. Twelve of those steps compound to exactly half, so holding KAS price flat, the daily payout to miners falls to about $29,315 a year from now and about $3,664 in four years. Fees, unchanged, would need to grow about 6,000-fold in a year and 750-fold in four just to stay this far behind, not to catch up.

Catching up would take real transaction volume. Holding today's $0.000063 median fee flat, matching today's $58,631 subsidy from fees alone needs about 10,771 transactions per second, sustained, not peak. That's roughly 12,000 times Kaspa's own measured 0.895 TPS, and higher than any sustained rate in this site's own comparison data, including Solana's 1,175. Holding today's transaction count flat instead, the same match needs a median fee near $0.76, about six times Bitcoin's own $0.1317 median. Neither looks close, and both would have to happen while the subsidy itself keeps shrinking underneath them.

This isn't unique to Kaspa: Bitcoin has run the same subsidy-funded deficit for its entire existence and remains the hardest chain to attack. But today, right now, Kaspa's security is bought almost entirely with inflation, not with people paying to use the chain, and no visible trend closes that gap on fees alone.

External cost

PoW security is bought with power, ASICs, and supply chains. That cost is real, and a fast chain does not make it go away.

Liquidity

Depth, custody, exchange support, and market makers do not follow from good design. They take years and money.

What would settle it

Users able to get in and out, hold their own keys, keep books, get help, and trade at depth, not just holders and volume existing.

Mining concentration

A small number of makers control ASIC supply. Pools cluster, and so does geography, so control can land in few hands even with an open protocol.

Fair-launch context

No premine does not mean no early concentration. The fair-launch comparison plots Kaspa's genesis supply against Ethereum, Cardano, and XRP, and what a fair launch does and doesn't guarantee.

Roadmap execution

Toccata activated on mainnet. vProgs, DAGKnight, and native DeFi are still separate tracks on separate timelines, and a testnet demo of one is not evidence for the rest. Treating them as one shipped bundle overstates how much is done.

What would settle it, and where roadmap talk outruns the evidence

A mainnet release and proof it turned on, with wallets, indexers, accepted transactions, and repeat flows, not a talk, branch, or testnet demo.

User demand

Low fees and spare room are needed for apps to exist. They are no proof that anyone will use them twice.

What would settle it

People coming back because an app does the job better on Kaspa than on a normal server, not low fees alone.

What to watch

How many separate node, API, and indexer operators exist. Whether roadmap items turn into apps with repeat users, not just demos. And the words: live, testnet, roadmap, and research get flattened into one story fastest in a hype cycle.
