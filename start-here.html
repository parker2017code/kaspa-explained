Start with the problem

Every crypto design answers one question: what breaks when you take away the trusted operator?

Take the bank out and its work does not vanish. Keys, agreement rules, rewards, and open markets grow back in its place, and each one arrives with a bill.
What "grows back" means, one line each

Keys stand in for a login: whoever holds the private key can move the balance, and nobody else can. Agreement rules replace the bank's internal ledger check: strangers running independent software still have to land on one shared history. Rewards replace payroll: something has to pay for the work of proposing and checking blocks. Open markets replace a teller window: getting a balance in or out means finding another party to trade with. No issuer stands behind it.

The starting problem

Why owning something digital is hard

A banknote cannot sit in two pockets at once; a file copies for nothing. Digital money has to build scarcity back out of rules. Most systems do that by naming an operator, a bank or an exchange that can freeze, reverse, or gate what you hold. A public crypto network replaces that operator with rules any participant can check alone.

What each option actually trades away

A bank or payment app gives support, reversals, and fraud handling, and costs you account freezes, operating hours, and jurisdiction limits. An exchange gives fast internal balances and an easy sign-up, and costs you custody risk and withdrawal risk. A public network gives rules nobody can bend for you personally, and costs you more moving parts and mistakes nobody can undo.

Summary: coins never move anywhere. A valid transaction changes an accepted record, and that record is the whole asset.

Proof without a login desk

Keys take over from the account

No operator means no password reset, so math stands in for both. A private key authorizes a spend; a signature proves the key holder approved this exact transaction. A hash is a fingerprint for data, changing completely if one byte does, which is how a block commits to the history behind it and a node can take the whole batch or none of it.

Getting there also takes agreement: thousands of independent computers see messages land in different orders and still have to settle on one shared history, ruling out theft, double-spends, and rewrites. Then it takes a cost, or fake history is free to fake; Kaspa's is Proof of Work.

Simple check: most tokens do not need to exist. Ask what this one does that BTC, ETH, USDC, or a plain database does not already do.

Two ways to track state

Balances or notes

An account model is a balance table: Alice has 10, sends 3, Alice has 7. Kaspa uses the UTXOUnspent transaction output. Value sits in separate coins, spent whole and replaced by new ones, instead of living in one account balance that gets edited. model instead, closer to cash notes: Alice spends a 10-coin note whole and gets a 7-coin note back as change. Spend a wallet on the UTXO-vs-accounts page and watch a UTXO get consumed once and replaced by new outputs.

Where Kaspa enters

Keep the mining, fix the ordering

Bitcoin-style chains pick one block path and throw the rest away, so two honest miners who solve a block seconds apart compete for one slot.
