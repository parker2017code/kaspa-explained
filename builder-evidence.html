How to prove a Kaspa builder claim

A reviewer believes your claim when they can check five things: the status label, the source, the artifact, the user-visible result, and the remaining blocker. All five, not a selection.

Use this for demo proof. Use Claims Checker for protocol status. Use Reality Check for app or product pitches before they become public claims.

Proof ladder

Claim

Source

Point to the strongest available source for that lane, not the most convenient one.

User-visible result

State what a wallet user, builder, miner, or operator can actually observe right now.

Remaining blocker

List what still stands between this and a broader public claim.

Evidence lanes

Kaspa claims split into mainnet features, near-term upgrades, and early design work. Each lane demands a different proof level.

  • Live: code merged, activated on mainnet, available to builders now
  • Targeted: scheduled for near-term release or being implemented
  • Roadmap: architecture approved or research-backed; scheduling still missing
  • Research: in exploration, no timeline or approval yet

Builder tooling, open code, and mainnet activation are three different states. Merged code proves the code exists. It doesn't prove mainnet activation.

Advanced state-transition proof note

State-transition proof models follow one pattern: selected-parent root plus current root plus leaf write witnesses. That keeps validation local to the single transition. Clients never have to maintain the whole incremental SMT.

Public builder paths

The concrete Kaspa capabilities you can rely on for product decisions:

Vault and escrow

Covenant rules enforce spending limits or unlock conditions at the script layer. No server in the loop to trust or compromise.

Receipts and proof of payment

Fast inclusion and ordered history mean a receipt settles quickly and verifies on-chain, not against a company's database. Indexers and APIs turn that into a queryable proof of payment.

Assurance contracts

A simple contract can enforce a group commitment: if N people agree, do X; otherwise, refund everyone. No coordinator holds the funds in between.

Coordination markets

Roadmap vProgs and oracle flows target markets where strangers agree on rules before they trust each other: prediction, funding, or work markets built on that base.

How to check a claim

  1. Find the claim in Claims Reference, or read its full context in Status.
  2. Check whether it's mainnet, testnet, future upgrade work, research, unsupported, or outside L1 scope: one of those six, not a blend.
  3. Follow the source link to primary material: code, releases, KIPs, papers, or core contributor posts.
  4. For builder tooling versus mainnet status: rusty-kaspa releases show code availability; docs.kaspa.org shows official builder guidance. They answer different questions.
  5. For roadmap claims: look for architecture documents or a stated research direction. An announcement post alone is weak evidence. It proves someone said something, not that it shipped.

Protocol status traces back to code, releases, and primary sources in Sources. Builder tooling status traces back to docs.kaspa.org and rusty-kaspa.
