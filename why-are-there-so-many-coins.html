Coin categories

Why there are many coins

Bitcoin sells scarcity, Ethereum sells programmable apps, stablecoins move dollars, and meme coins trade attention. They were never competing for the same job, and that's why there are thousands of coins instead of one winner.

One list, many instruments

Sort a ranking page by market cap and BTC, ETH, USDT, XRP, BNB, SOL, DOGE, LINK, LTC, BCH, XMR, wrapped assets, stablecoins, and meme tokens all land in one column. Same units, same table, completely different products: a scarcity play, a fee-driven platform, a dollar substitute, and an attention market, ranked as if they compete for the same job.

Why new coins appear

Different reasons coins exist

Different design answers

PoW versus PoS, UTXO versus account model, privacy versus transparency, execution speed versus low node requirements, L1 versus L2. Each pick trades away something the other kept, so no single design wins on every axis at once.

Different social goals

Digital gold, peer-to-peer cash, private payments, DeFi apps, enterprise settlement, exchange ecosystems, games, identity, storage, coordination.

Forks and disagreements

Communities split when they can't agree on scaling, governance, monetary rules, or what the project is even supposed to be.

Speculation incentives

A new token attracts liquidity and attention on its own, independent of whether the network needed a token at all.

First question

Ask what the coin is trying to be

Money or settlement asset? App platform? Dollar token? Exchange ecosystem token? Privacy tool? Governance token? Infrastructure network? Attention market? Claim on an off-chain asset?

Why value differs

High or low market value can come from many causes

Why a coin may be highly valued

  • Deep liquidity and exchange access.
  • A narrative simple enough to repeat in one sentence.
  • A large developer or app ecosystem.
  • A community with real social consensus behind it.
  • Staking, fee, collateral, or security demand.
  • Institutional familiarity or regulatory clarity.
  • Speculative attention and leverage.

Why good tech may stay low-valued

  • Weak liquidity or exchange access.
  • No narrative simple enough to spread.
  • A token that fails to capture the value the network creates.
  • A small developer ecosystem.
  • Funding and business-development gaps.
  • Bad timing, bad UX, or stronger competitors.
  • A market that hasn't priced the tradeoff yet.
