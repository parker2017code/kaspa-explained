What is live?

Parallel blocks, one history

Kaspa is real-time Proof of Work.

It keeps mined Proof of Work and a UTXO model, then uses a blockDAG and GHOSTDAG so honest blocks can be found in parallel and still ordered into one shared history.

Kaspa's core move is simple: do not force honest work to wait in a single-file chain when it can stay in the graph and still be ordered into one shared payment history.

Real-time does not mean instant finality. It means fast mined inclusion and user feel; confirmation confidence is still probabilistic.

What matters first.

Kaspa is a mined UTXO blockDAG using GHOSTDAG. The live network is the Crescendo-era 10 BPS chain: people can send, receive, self-custody, mine, run nodes, use wallets and explorers, and try ecosystem-token workflows around KRC tooling.

Toccata, vProgs, DAGKnight, and richer app rails are separate build stages. Keep them in view, but do not bundle them into the live payment rail. When the exact boundary matters, use the status page.

Read numbers carefully

Speed is not one number.

Kaspa is fast. Block rate, confirmation confidence, throughput, and finality measure different parts of that claim.

Block rate

10 BPS is live. It improves payment feel; finality remains probabilistic.

TPS

Capacity tests, average sustained capacity, policy limits, fees, and organic demand are different measurements. Say which one you mean.

Why Kaspa

Why Kaspa gets attention.

Kaspa is interesting where people need neutral money, self-custody, fast payment feel, and later app rules that do not depend on one company changing the database.

Read why Kaspa matters

Why it gets attention.

Kaspa keeps mined work and lowers the latency of the user experience. Honest blocks found at nearly the same time can remain in the graph, and GHOSTDAG gives nodes a rule for ordering that graph.

The app thesis comes later: apps that prove rules, publish receipts, enforce spend constraints, or coordinate commitments while the base layer focuses on ordering payments and commitments. The hard question is adoption: durable usage, liquidity, fees, and builders at production scale.

One visual idea

Bitcoin chooses one path. Kaspa orders parallel work.

A normal blockchain has one selected chain. When honest miners find blocks at nearly the same time, one path wins and the other competing blocks usually become wasted side blocks. Kaspa's blockDAG starts from a different shape: honest parallel blocks can reference one another and GHOSTDAG gives nodes a rule for ordering that graph.
