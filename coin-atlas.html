Trust model

Sort assets by what you have to trust

No single issuer, PoW-style money

BTC, LTC, BCH, XMR, and KAS have no company behind the token: miners secure the network directly. The risk lives in mining economics, node validation, liquidity depth, and whether the security budget holds up as issuance drops.

Smart-contract platforms

ETH, SOL, AVAX, SUI, ADA, and BNB Chain sell programmable state. The token pays for compute and storage; it doesn't function as money on its own. The risk is execution scale under load, validator concentration, bridge exploits, and whether developers keep shipping there next year.

Issuer-backed assets

USDT, USDC, PYUSD, tokenized gold, and tokenized stocks are only as good as the entity standing behind them. Reserves, redemption rights, banking access, and jurisdiction sit upstream of the token: those are what to check.

Governance and protocol tokens

UNI, AAVE, MKR/SKY, COMP, and CRV vote on protocol parameters, treasuries, or fees. One question decides whether that vote is worth anything: does the governance control real economic value, or just settings nobody profits from changing?

Wrapped and liquid staking assets

WBTC, cbBTC, stETH, rETH, and JitoSOL are claims on an underlying asset held somewhere else. Every layer of composability adds a layer of risk: custodian, bridge, smart-contract bug, depeg, rehypothecation.

Attention assets

A meme coin is worth something because attention itself trades. That's the entire mechanism: no protocol, no cash flow, no redemption right underneath it.

Kaspa bridge

Where Kaspa fits in the atlas

Kaspa sits in the PoW monetary-network branch with Bitcoin, Litecoin, and Bitcoin Cash, but it drops the single-chain block structure. Blocks form a DAG instead of a chain, and GHOSTDAG orders them: that ordering mechanism is behind Kaspa's faster confirmation feel.

It is not a stablecoin, an exchange token, a meme coin, or a live smart-contract platform. Judge it on the terms that apply to a PoW money network: mining security, fair launch, blockDAG throughput, liquidity depth, node requirements, and whether mined payments that settle faster than Bitcoin actually solve a problem someone has.
