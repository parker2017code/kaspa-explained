The Instrument

Moose spent three challenges building one book: write a compressed instrument for judging monetary systems, then explain it slowly enough that the reasoning shows, then point it at things it was never built to measure. It runs 279 pages. It is hosted here in full, free, at his request.

Read the book (PDF, 279 pages) Moose on X

Whose claims these are: this is Moose's argument, published as he wrote it. It is not part of this site's checked claim set, it has not been run through the claims checker, and nothing in it was edited here. Read it as a serious piece of thinking by one author, which is what it is.

What it argues

Cost as the thing you can actually measure

The instrument starts from physics rather than economics. A joule floor: every unit of security bought with proof of work costs energy that cannot be faked, refunded, or voted into existence. From there Moose builds a working vocabulary out of electrical and thermodynamic parts, voltage and current and resistance, impedance, a Brownian ratchet and a Feynman ratchet, and uses them to ask one question of any monetary channel: what in it is genuinely fixed, and what only looks fixed until something leans on it.

Applied to proof of stake, the reading is critical, and Moose is careful about how. He states plainly that none of it requires believing proof of stake is a scam, or that the people building on it act in bad faith, or that the utility inside those ecosystems is not real. The argument is narrower: a system whose security traces back to existing token ownership has a different floor from one whose security traces back to spent energy, and the difference shows up under load.

Part Three is where it gets interesting even if you disagree with Part One. The same instrument gets pointed at problems it was not designed for, which is the honest test of whether a framework is doing work or just restating its own assumptions.

Why it is here

Hosting, not endorsing

This site keeps a hard line between what is verified and what is argued. Almost everything on it carries a status label and a source, and the source hierarchy decides what settles a claim. A book is a different kind of object. It makes an argument, and an argument is judged by whether it holds together, not by whether a repository confirms it.

So the line is drawn here instead of inside the text: this page hosts Moose's work, credits him, and changes nothing. If you want the site's own positions, with sources attached, the status page and the claims checker are where those live.
