Market value

Why crypto has value

A token price is smoke; the question is what's burning underneath it. Sometimes that's fee demand, collateral demand, and a security budget somebody is paying for. Sometimes it's culture, borrowed money, and insiders walking out through the order book.

Price

Why cryptoassets get prices

Demand for use

Someone needs the asset itself: to pay fees, settle a trade, stake, post collateral, or get into an app.

Monetary premium

Markets pay extra for credible scarcity, liquidity, durability, and censorship resistance backed by a rule set people trust enough not to check twice.

Security economics

Higher rewards buy more mining or staking security, and a higher market value makes the network a more tempting target to attack or capture.

Speculation

Markets price in expectations, narrative, attention, and leverage. Price rises because people expect price to rise; that loop can run for a long time before it needs a reason.

Rule: a price pump doesn't prove adoption, and a high market cap doesn't prove usefulness. A technically sound project can still be overpriced.

The number everyone quotes

Market cap is not company value

Market cap is price times circulating supply, nothing more. That much cash never had to flow in, no revenue sits behind the number, and nobody could sell the whole supply at that price.

Price per coin means nothing without supply attached. Circulating supply can differ sharply from total supply. Low float inflates paper valuations without a matching amount of real capital. Volume can mean liquidity, hype, wash trading, or panic: the number alone doesn't say which. Social dominance measures attention. Fundamentals need their own evidence. Fully diluted value shows the dilution still coming.

Kaspa/BTC example: one KAS costs less than one BTC simply because there are far more of them in circulation. Compare market cap, liquidity, access, adoption, security history, and execution risk instead of unit price.

Who benefits, who gets hurt

Open markets and their costs

Why markets help

  • Miners and validators can sell rewards.
  • Users can acquire the fee asset.
  • Apps can price collateral and liquidity.
  • Global participants can enter and exit without one issuer.
  • Security budget gets a market signal.

What markets break

  • Pump-and-dumps become easy to coordinate.
  • Insiders can use retail liquidity as exit liquidity.
  • Leverage can force violent liquidations.
  • Narrative can outrun working technology.
  • Price crowds out every other thing a newcomer could learn first.

Ponzi or not

Risk and fraud

Trading in an open market where early buyers profit does not make an asset a Ponzi. What does: promising guaranteed returns, paying yield out of new deposits instead of revenue, hiding who holds the supply, or building something that mainly exists so insiders have someone to sell to.

Not enough to call fraud

Volatility, early gains, speculation, or the absence of an obvious consumer app.

Red flags

Guaranteed yield, hidden insiders, vague utility, unlock cliffs, fake partnerships, opaque reserves, no working system.

Price is not legitimacy

A meme can reach a large valuation on attention alone. A technically sound network can sit ignored for years.

Launch design

Initial ownership shapes trust

Launch modelWhat it improvesWhat it risks
Fair launch / mined launchLower official insider-allocation risk.No built-in treasury, early-miner advantage, harder funding.
PremineFunds development, legal work, grants, audits, and integrations.Insider control, sell pressure, trust problems.
Public sale or ICOFast fundraising and broad early buyer base.Regulatory risk, hype buying, weak product discipline.
VC allocationProfessional funding and business support.Unlock overhang and retail exit-liquidity risk.
AirdropRewards users and broadens distribution.Farming, sybil attacks, immediate sell pressure.

A fair launch removes the official allocation table. It does not remove the funding, coordination, early-miner, liquidity, or ecosystem-development questions that a premine would have paid for.
