Crypto design has tradeoffs

No crypto design gets speed, security, decentralization, and privacy all at once. Push on one and the cost shows up somewhere else: node requirements, latency, regulatory exposure, or who actually controls the network.

Who checks the work

Why crypto differs from Visa

Visa settles fast because Visa doesn't ask for independent verification. Trusted institutions, legal agreements, one company's infrastructure, and fraud teams do the work instead. A public blockchain asks many independent participants to check the same rules without trusting any single operator. That redundant verification is the bottleneck Visa doesn't have.

Normal app or payment network

Runs on infrastructure one company controls. That operator can optimize for speed, reverse errors, block users, and change rules whenever it wants.

Public crypto base layer

Independent nodes can reject invalid history without asking permission. The cost of that guarantee: data, execution, and ordering all have to stay verifiable by people outside any one company.

Beginner summary: crypto can look Visa-fast at the app or custody layer. A decentralized base layer can't, without quietly picking up Visa's trust assumptions along the way.

Data exposure

Privacy and public verification conflict

Public chains expose transaction data because independent verifiers need that data to check the rules. Hiding sender, receiver, amount, or app state solves privacy and creates new cost: heavier cryptography, bigger data, harder verification, thinner liquidity, and more regulatory friction.

Transparent ledgers

Easy to audit supply and verify rules. Bad for user privacy and business confidentiality.

Privacy-first systems

Better fungibility, less surveillance. Harder exchange access, heavier data, more regulatory pressure.

Shielding has limits

Worth having. It doesn't remove the scaling, compliance, UX, and verification costs underneath it.

Hardware

ASICs and staking are different concentration pressures

ASIC mining

Specialized machines put real-world cost into PoW security: electricity, hardware, facilities, cooling, supply chains, geography, time. That cost doesn't prevent concentration. It just moves it to whoever controls capital, cheap energy, chip manufacturing, firmware, and pools.

Staking

PoS skips industrial hardware entirely: the security resource is token ownership itself. Concentration risk moves accordingly, to large holders, staking providers, exchanges, liquid-staking protocols, and governance votes.

Kaspa's tradeoff is fast PoW

Kaspa's specific bet: keep PoW and UTXO design while using a blockDAG and GHOSTDAG ordering to include parallel honest blocks instead of discarding them, improving confirmation feel. That bet does not cancel node economics, mining economics, app-layer challenges, privacy tradeoffs, market volatility, or roadmap execution risk. They still apply, same as any other chain.
