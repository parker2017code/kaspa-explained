Why six dials, and not ten

  1. This was ten dials until 29 August 2026. Across the ten figures, the first principal component carries 42.8% of the variance and the effective dimensionality is 5.26 of 10: worth about six questions, not ten.

  2. Six is the smallest grouping that holds up, tested over 6,000 random dial settings against an 80% floor set before the check ran.

Coverage, and estimating a figure a board never published

  1. Two coverage gates. A model needs at least 10 figures to enter the roster, and 4 of the 10 scored figures to be ranked. A model that passes and is still thin says so on its own row.

  2. One figure carries more of the picker than any other, and ten models don't have it: ARC-AGI-2 is published for 13 of the 23 models here, and the other 10 are filled by regression.

  3. A missing figure is estimated from whatever correlates with it, weighted by how much that correlate explains. Across the grid, 25 of 230 cells are estimated this way and 29 more carried across from another effort setting.

  4. The estimates were tested by hiding real answers. A prediction lands 12.5 points off on average, and 94.6% of the held-out misses fell inside the interval the page itself declared. Price and speed are never estimated.

  5. Every estimated cell is marked on its own row: see the full 23×10 table for which ones, measured, sibling-carried, or imputed.

One row per model, at one point on its own effort curve

  1. Each model ships once, at the setting worth running, not its highest score: capability minus eight points for every doubling of price.

  2. Every model is placed at the same point on its own price ladder, so one lab's max effort is never held against another's medium: 0.40 of the way up each model's own price span, refitted each rebuild.

    That is the thinnest number on this page. Pooled across both boards the knee lands at 0.40, but a 4,000-sample bootstrap over the six families with real priced rungs puts it at 0.30, with a 95 percent interval from 0.20 to 0.40.

  3. Price, not response time, is the ruler: held out one family at a time, price misses by 0.167 predicting what a rung buys against 0.260 for the clock.

  4. A model with two or more priced settings is read straight off its own curve; the rest borrow the shared curve, with a wider, stated error.

What isn't scored

  1. Style, on LM Arena. Its Style Control setting strips formatting and length; the four Arena text figures here use it. Across 33 models the median model loses 10 points when style is removed, so most of that board is partly a writing contest. Claude Opus 5 is the exception, gaining 16.

  2. Cost. It is the horizontal axis of the value chart on the picker and nothing else. Scoring it as a seventh dial would count a cheap model's cheapness twice.

  3. Speed. On the honest scale, time to first token separates the top five by 0.3 points and total response time by 0.9. A dial that cannot separate the leaders is ranking noise with a decimal point on it.
