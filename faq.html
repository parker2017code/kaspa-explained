FAQ

Kaspa questions, answered

Answers come first. When one depends on evidence, the link takes you to the status page or source so you can check it yourself.

Live network

What is live now?

Is Kaspa Proof of Work?

Yes. Kaspa is mined Proof of Work, not Proof of Stake.

What is GHOSTDAG?

GHOSTDAG is Kaspa's live consensus rule for ordering a blockDAG so parallel honest blocks still count toward consensus.

What does 10 BPS mean?

Roughly ten blocks per second in the Crescendo-era network. Throughput and finality still run into their own protocol limits.

Common misconceptions

Short corrections for claims that spread fast

Does 10 BPS prove a fixed TPS number?

No. TPS depends on workload and transaction shape. Simple payments currently run roughly 2.5k to 3.4k TPS; covenant apps, ZK settlements, tokens, and vProgs each need their own measurement context. See Kaspa TPS Explained.

Does 10 BPS or real-time mean instant finality?

No. Faster blocks improve inclusion, capacity, and user feel. Confirmation confidence is a separate question, and it stays probabilistic either way.

Do testnet numbers count as mainnet?

No. Testnets and experiments are development evidence. Mainnet guarantees need mainnet evidence.

Does code activity mean activation?

No. Branches, pull requests, demos, and talks show progress, not activation. Activation needs a primary release, KIP, node, or network confirming it.

Does low fee usage prove adoption?

Not by itself. Low fees just make experiments cheap. Adoption needs durable wallets, integrations, liquidity, apps, recurring users, and fee-paying demand.

Why is one KAS so much cheaper than one BTC?

Unit price and valuation are different things. Bitcoin has fewer units and a far larger market cap, and the market prices it as a proven monetary network with deep liquidity, institutional access, custody, derivatives, and a global brand. Kaspa trades more like a promising technical network that still has to prove durable adoption beyond its architecture.

Could that mean Kaspa is underpriced?

Only if the market is discounting future adoption too heavily. The bullish case: fast Proof of Work, Toccata, based-app development, and real products eventually create demand the market hasn't priced in yet. The skeptical case: Bitcoin already has monetary certainty, while Kaspa still carries execution, liquidity, wallet, app, and adoption risk.

Does a roadmap idea belong in the headline?

Usually not. Lead with what works now, then say plainly what still depends on future upgrades.

Roadmap and research

Still open

Is Toccata live?

Yes. Toccata activated at DAA 474,165,565. Rusty Kaspa v2.0.1 is the current Toccata release; v2.0.0 set the activation parameters. Use Toccata status for post-activation checks.

Are Kaspa smart contracts live?

No mature Kaspa-native programmability on mainnet yet. The near-term path runs through Toccata: spend rules, asset rules, ZK proof checks, sequencing commitments, then later vProgs.

Does Toccata allow one action across multiple apps?

Only partly. Atomicity is the boundary: two transactions landing near each other is weaker than one combined operation that succeeds or fails as a unit. Toccata gives Kaspa covenant, ZK, sequencing, and based-zk foundations for fast verifiable coordination. Full app-to-app atomic actions belong to the later vProgs roadmap.

Can a ZK proof prove what happened on another chain?

Not by itself. A proof shows that a circuit checked against chosen inputs. A bridge or oracle-style app still needs an agreed anchor for the outside fact: a source-chain light client, finality certificate, accumulated-work view, oracle, reporter set, or challenge process.

Does Kaspa need a based rollup for the first Toccata apps?

No. Direct L1 covenant examples like vaults, escrow, and assurance contracts can be built on Toccata itself. A based rollup becomes relevant later for richer execution, especially where the path stays compatible with vProgs.

Is DAGKnight live?

No. DAGKnight is a parameterless/adaptive consensus upgrade direction. Current mainnet consensus is GHOSTDAG.

Are vProgs live?

No. vProgs are roadmap architecture for apps that prove richer rules while sharing Kaspa ordering. Live app infrastructure needs activation and product evidence first.

Does Toccata mean external L2s are required?

No. The thesis is L1-first: Toccata puts covenant and based-zk rails on Kaspa L1 itself, and apps or runtimes build on that shared ordering and payment surface. Full vProgs later target app-to-app composition beyond standalone based-zk apps.

Is native Kaspa DeFi live?

No. Native DeFi is still roadmap/application architecture, waiting on future foundations and tooling.

Are RTD-derived oracle or TangVM flows live?

No. Base RTD is Hashdag's real-time PoW framing for Kaspa. Oracles, TangVM, miner attestations, and public group-commitment markets are app-level research ideas built over Kaspa primitives, and product claims need their own implementation evidence.

Does DAGKnight use observed latency?

The research framing uses adversarial or worst-case latency assumptions: conditions rougher than the smooth latency seen during normal network operation.

What does Solana-like mean?

A cohesive, Rust-friendly developer and user experience. Kaspa L1 keeps its own execution model regardless.

Comparison

How should Kaspa be described?

Is Kaspa just Bitcoin but faster?

No. Better framing: Kaspa is Proof of Work redesigned around parallel blocks. It keeps mined security but orders competing blocks through a blockDAG and GHOSTDAG instead of picking one chain.

Does Kaspa solve every scaling problem?

No. The live claim is narrower: faster Proof-of-Work inclusion and confirmation dynamics through a blockDAG. Storage, node resources, fees, and future app infrastructure are all still open work.

Does Kaspa require super-fast internet?

Current GHOSTDAG carries latency assumptions, like other synchronous consensus systems. DAGKnight is the future research direction for removing a fixed protocol latency parameter, but it isn't live today.

Does pruning make Kaspa private?

No. Pruning and missing explorer history aren't privacy guarantees. UTXO state remains the consensus object, and broadcast transactions can still be observed or archived by others.
