# V5 advanced examples: bounded feasibility review

Reviewed local primary sources at Argent `d08e52dd1e18c9f7e9a2dc482048c2db31d25611` and SilverScript `c7d17a15ac88610d013ec9ffffa9520aeb69929b`. The table began as a design assessment. Two selected examples, the fixed ring and bonded courier, are now implemented and locally verified; the other eight remain proposals. “Supported” means the required primitive is present in the pinned source. Current implementation and acceptance evidence is collected in [V5 local acceptance](v5-local-acceptance.md). Effort is relative to the existing V5 builder, wallet review, acceptance journal and scene.

Whole supply chain in one transaction | Named consumed/observed states and constrained outputs can make several digital inventory and payment transitions succeed together [A]. | New production actor must enforce each recipe and all intermediate/final quantities. The sample is not a dividend implementation. External revenue remains outside enforcement until deposited. | High |
| 4. Commit/reveal auction | `sha256`/`blake2b`, message/transaction signatures, and absolute/relative locks exist [F,G]. | Need commitment domain binding, bid/refund states, reveal-secret recovery, tie rules and bounded settlement. | 5. Signed receipt pays payment + bond to the courier; an age-eligible refund forfeits both to the customer. Recipient signature attests receipt; it does not independently prove physical delivery. Refund eligibility is not automatic execution or priority over a valid release. Exact payouts, replay rejection and actual consensus age boundaries are locally tested. | **Moderate; implemented** |
| 6. Offchain signed channel with challenge | Explicit digest signatures can authorize sequence-numbered balance states; a relative lock can gate final settlement [F,G]. | Need channel/domain binding, latest-state durable storage, higher-sequence challenge logic, exact payout conservation, watcher availability and fee recovery. Existing bilateral Business trade and `clearThreeParty` game simulation remain separate [B]. The displayed goods are game-issued resources, and the demo host controls the role keys. | **Low–moderate; implemented** |
| 8. | 9. Bounded scheduling with verified reward | Existing Business policy decrements per-resource/native allowances and checks a minimum resource receipt [B]. ## Selected manageable scope

The implemented examples are **a fixed three-party circular swap** and **bonded courier release/refund**. The ring exchanges three digital inventories under three distinct owner signatures in one transaction. The courier uses a disclosed recipient signature for release and relative DAA age for refund eligibility. The demo service controls these signing roles; distinct keys are not a claim of independent people. A third example remains a possible extension only if its result predicate is small and independently checked onchain, such as an exact shipment allocation.

Keep the whole-supply-chain scene for a later extension: adding genuine resource transformation introduces additional issuance/consumption invariants. Do not present sequential physical work as atomic merely because its game records settle together.

Both selected designs now have pinned artifacts, exact SDK builders, independently reconstructed plans, and valid/adversarial VM fixtures. The courier refund builder explicitly sets the input sequence to the contract’s relative DAA age; ordinary Business and Ring inputs retain their zero-sequence policy [I]. Accepted Testnet records and the remaining public deployment gate are tracked separately in [local acceptance](v5-local-acceptance.md). Linking checks still have documented handle/interface gaps; these examples use pinned, locally compiled concrete actors rather than arbitrary foreign artifacts [J].
