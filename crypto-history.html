History

Crypto history as problem solving

Every era of crypto is a workaround for the limit the era before it ran into. Read the timeline that way and each fix names the problem it inherited.

Problem timeline

Problems by era

Before Bitcoin

Digital cash

Digital data copies for free. Every pre-Bitcoin proposal had to fake the scarcity that atoms get automatically.

Bitcoin

Double-spending

Proof of Work made rewriting a shared payment history expensive enough that no financial institution had to referee it.

Early mining

Security market

Mining rewards turned CPUs into GPUs, GPUs into ASICs, and solo miners into pools and industrial operations chasing the same margin.

Litecoin and forks

Parameter experiments

Communities forked Bitcoin's code to change one dial at a time: block times, mining algorithm, or scaling philosophy.

Ethereum

Programmability

A ledger that runs code: shared state any application could read and write, well beyond simple balances.

2017

ICOs and scaling war

Fundraising ran ahead of working products while Bitcoin's own community split over how to scale it.

DeFi and stablecoins

On-chain finance

Dollar tokens, exchanges, lending, collateral, and automated market makers turned early demos into real, ongoing usage.

Rollups and fast L1s

Scale and UX

Execution moved off the base chain, into L2s, high-throughput L1s, appchains, and new consensus designs built to avoid the same bottleneck twice.

Forks

Software plus social agreement

A company ships an update and users get it whether they like it or not. Crypto has no such lever: users, miners or validators, exchanges, wallets, and businesses all have to independently choose to run the new rules. A fork is what happens when they stop converging.

Soft-fork style change

Tightens the rules. Upgraded nodes enforce new restrictions while un-upgraded nodes still see the resulting blocks as valid.

Hard-fork style change

Changes the rules in a way old software rejects outright. Sometimes planned and boring, sometimes the moment a community splits in two.

What conservatism costs

Bitcoin as the reference point

Bitcoin's value case rests on fixed-supply culture, PoW security, liquidity, exchange support, over a decade of surviving attacks, conservative upgrades, and a monetary story simple enough to repeat in one sentence. The tradeoff for that conservatism: no fast retail-speed L1 UX, no expressive smart contracts, no easy experimentation.

Why other chains exist

Different projects, different pressures

Bitcoin Cash

Split off over one number: block size. Bigger blocks, cheaper payments, more centralization risk.

Ethereum

Bet that programmable state was worth giving up Bitcoin's minimalism.

Solana-style chains

Bet that consumer-app speed was worth trading away low node requirements.

Monero and Zcash

Bet that privacy was worth the heavier cryptography and the regulatory scrutiny that follows it.

Stablecoins

Bet that a dollar claim backed by an issuer or a collateral pool beats trusting a central bank's monetary policy.

Kaspa

Kept PoW and UTXO but replaced the single chain with a blockDAG, betting that ordering parallel blocks beats discarding them.
