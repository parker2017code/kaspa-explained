Toccata status

Personal essay · Part 3 of 3

Fees, mining, and what it adds up to

Toccata is plumbing in the good sense: proof room, spam cost, solo-mining paths, source boundaries, and a stronger sovereignty story.

By Parker Schmidt (@parker2017)

The fee and block-size changes

Two smaller changes ride in with Toccata, and both are constantly getting misread.

First, the transient-mass byte allowance roughly doubles, from about 125 KB to 250 KB. This is mostly about making room for STARK proofs. A proof can be around 225 KB, so a block needs enough byte room to hold one plus the surrounding transaction data. No room for the proof, no ZK verification on L1. Simple as that.

Second, a minimum standard fee. Doubling the byte allowance means the DAG can soak up more data, which increases the disk burden on people running nodes, archives, and explorers. Real activity filling that space is fine. For a service or app, the fee is small compared with the service being purchased, settled, or protected. For someone filling blocks with junk, the floor makes chain filling cost money.

The minimum fee is a mempool policy rule. It is app and service infrastructure policy, not base consensus. Consensus-valid and standard-relayed are different claims. A miner can choose to accept cheaper transactions, but most miners run the default policy, and only that miner's share of blocks would carry cheaper spam. The floor can also be lowered later with an ordinary software release if KAS price or network conditions make that sensible.

This is about a floor, not a full fee market. The point is simple: make proofs fit, and make bulk junk cost something.

The Rust Stratum Bridge

This one is wired straight into the sovereignty story, and imo, it is underrated. Shoutout to @LiveLaughLove13, who built the in-house Rust Stratum Bridge that is now part of rusty-kaspa and currently in beta.

The sovereignty pitch usually stops at "run your own node." For proof of work, the better version is "mine to your own node." Problem is ASICs do not speak Kaspa's node language. They speak Stratum, so for a long time you needed a separate bridge or, more commonly, you handed the job to a pool because wiring yourself directly into the network was a pain. Pulling a maintained Stratum bridge into the Rust Kaspa package itself makes solo mining a normal thing instead of a weekend science project. Run your own node, verify your own chain, mine to your own node. Be your own bank, be your own miner.

An implication came up that I had not chewed on before, and I would flag it as speculation because the mechanism is not decided: if RTD-style miner attestations or oracle flows eventually need miners to run extra software and attest to outside claims on chain, then a miner-side plug-in path could matter. This is app responsibility, and it is not base consensus responsibility. Maybe nothing. The direction is interesting.

What it adds up to

Covenants make UTXOs expressive. Covenant IDs keep that expressiveness honest. ZK moves the expensive computation off chain while keeping cheap verification on it. KIP-21 lets apps find their own activity instead of scanning the DAG universe. The fee and byte-room changes make room for proofs without making spam free. The Stratum Bridge makes solo mining, and maybe future miner-side app jobs, easier to reason about. Stack those up and the sovereignty story gets more complete: sovereign money, sovereign verification, sovereign mining, sovereign apps, and maybe one day a sovereign way to bring real-world information in.

This still is not completely finished. vProgs are not here. DAGKnight is not here. 100 BPS is not here. RTD is not here. Toccata is not the final form. It is also not nothing, which is the thing Kaspa people keep doing to themselves: under-celebrating what shipped now because we are often squinting at the next milestone. Toccata is not just a roadmap promise anymore. It is live primitives once activation completes. Someone can wake up after activation and build something that was flat-out impossible the day before: a vault, an asset, a weird state channel, a ZK app proving its work back to L1, a chess game, or something none of us have thought of yet.

For years, UTXO programmability has been this quiet research corner full of brilliant people inventing clever workarounds. Bitcoin has some of the sharpest minds in the entire space, and a lot of them are stuck working around a culture that does not want the solution to exist on L1. The same little opcode that quietly makes most of this possible, OP_CAT, is something Bitcoin has refused for years on purpose, because it treats that much expressiveness as a risk, both to keeping coins as pure money and to Bitcoin's own security. That is a real philosophy, and not necessarily a dumb one. Kaspa made a different call, and tried hard to keep the lean, verifiable, sovereign properties that made this whole thing worth caring about in the first place.

That is why I keep landing on the word elegant, because none of this has been, is, or will be easy. It took the Rust rewrite, Crescendo, years of grinding performance work, and a set of upgrades and tools most users will never need to know by name: the new opcodes, covenant IDs, the ZK verifier, KIP-21's activity tracking, SilverScript, Argent, and whatever core is already working on to make this future more usable.

Which is the real reason I am bullish, more than any chart. The design is good. The people are better. Core devs, contributors like @LiveLaughLove13, the SilverScript and tooling folks, the writers and explainers, people like me, hello, trying to make this legible to humans who do not read consensus code for fun. We are a real community doing hard, unglamorous work because this design goal is worth protecting.

Underneath the coin, even underneath the people, there is this idea I have actually fallen for. You take something simple and clean, a lock, a key, one rule about what is allowed to come next, and you arrange those tiny pieces into something complex and genuinely beautiful. That is the whole story of computing. It is the story of covenants. It is the thing Bitcoin's best researchers wanted for years and were not allowed to have. Kaspa went and built it.

Status and sources

Activation

Toccata is released in Rusty Kaspa v2.0.1. Mainnet activation is scheduled at DAA score 474,165,565.

KIPs

KIP-16, KIP-17, KIP-20, and KIP-21 cover the ZK, covenant, covenant-ID, and sequencing-commitment pieces.

Tooling

SilverScript and Argent are public Michael Sutton repos. They are early developer tooling, not polished consumer products.

Primary links: Rusty Kaspa v2.0.1, Toccata guide, KIP-16, KIP-17, KIP-20, KIP-21, SilverScript, and Argent.

Back to where I started

It is already here, or close enough to watch without turning the next thing into the only thing. You do not have to wait for the next milestone to feel something about this one. Maybe that is the actual practice, in crypto and otherwise: notice the thing in front of you, and sit with it for a second.

This is worth basking in.
