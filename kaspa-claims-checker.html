Kaspa claims checker

Most bad Kaspa claims blend testnet work, roadmap design, and live mainnet behavior into one sentence. The table below pulls them apart.

Page scope, and when this table was last checked

This page covers protocol claims. Product and app pitches go to Status. A demo, txid, repo, or testnet artifact offered as proof goes to Build on Kaspa.

Common claims

Live, roadmap, research, or wrong?

Grouped so a status is visible at a glance. Open a claim for the reasoning and the source.

Core protocol

ClaimStatusWhy
Kaspa is proof-of-workLive
Why

Kaspa is mined, proof-of-work, full stop. Source: rusty-kaspa.

Kaspa is a blockDAG using GHOSTDAGLive
Why

GHOSTDAG orders parallel blocks into one history instead of discarding the losers. Source: GHOSTDAG page.

Kaspa is 10 BPSLive
Why

10 blocks per second, the Crescendo-era rate. Source: releases.

Kaspa already runs at 100 BPSResearch
Why not

10 BPS is live. 100 BPS is an unconfirmed roadmap proposal with no release, KIP, or code behind it. The 100 BPS, sub-40ms-block proposal exists only in kaspa.org's roadmap prose, which this site does not treat as a citable source after it was caught describing Toccata as pre-mainnet eight weeks after mainnet activation. Source: unverified, flagged.

Kaspa payments become irreversible immediatelyWrong
Why

Inclusion is fast; confirmation confidence rises probabilistically over time. Source: finality page.

Toccata and smart contracts

ClaimStatusWhy
Toccata has activated on mainnetLive protocol
Why

Live, activated at DAA 474,165,565; v2.0.1 followed as the release. App, wallet, explorer, SDK, and user adoption are separate, still-open checks. Source: Toccata page.

TN10 Toccata test means mainnet activationTestnet only
Why not

No. tn10-toc2, tn10-toc3, and the Testnet-10 DAA evidence are testnet-only; v2.0.0 set the mainnet activation score. Source: v2.0.0 activation release.

TN12 proves mainnet covenantsTestnet only
What actually proves it

Covenants are live on mainnet, but TN12 is not why. That claim rests on the v2.0.0 activation score; TN12 is testnet prototyping evidence. Source: status.

Kaspa has mature native smart contractsNot yet
What's still ahead

Toccata shipped the covenant and proof-check layer only. Mature native DeFi and full app infrastructure are still ahead, through later vProgs work. Source: smart-contract status.

Tokenomics and standards

ClaimStatusWhy
July 2026 is a sudden emission cliffMisleading
Why

Emission steps down by a fixed factor every 2,629,800 seconds, triggered by DAA score, not the calendar: 24.49971474 KAS/sec from 478,611,000, then 23.12465141 from 504,909,000, which the network passed around August 5 and where it still sits as of August 22, then 21.82676446 from 531,207,000, roughly early September. Source: tokenomics.

KCC-0020 is a ratified fungible-token standardNot yet
The defect and the one production implementer

KCC-0001, KCC-0002, and KCC-0020 merged into the kccs repo, but each still reads Status: Draft in its own body. KCC-0020 also carries a permanent-supply-split consequence (issue #14): its transfer-consolidation rule and its extended-state update entrypoint contradict each other, so a token using the extended-state path can permanently split its own supply. Co-authors defend one pattern behind this as intended, a frozen or blacklist flag, but conceded a second pattern, ephemeral state that mutates without affecting fungibility, is a wrong one needing a spec fix. The reporter's concern stands unwithdrawn, and the issue remains open with no spec edit. KaspaCom, the one production implementer on record, calls its own deployed artifact "partially aligned, not fully conforming." Source: kccs repo.

KCC-0021 and KCC-0402 are adopted standards already running in productionWrong
Why not

Both are open, unmerged pull requests, not merged specs. Their PR bodies claim production use (kascov, KaspaRocket, a k402 reference implementation on mainnet); those claims live inside the open proposals themselves and could not be independently confirmed. Source: kccs repo.

Roadmap and app layer

ClaimStatusWhy
DAGKnight is current mainnet consensusResearch
Where the branch stands

No. Mainnet ordering runs GHOSTDAG; DAGKnight has a paper and a prototype branch, not a merge. It is the consensus upgrade after Toccata. What it does not have is a merge: KIP-2 still reads Status: Proposed, even though the branch itself is active, with commits as recent as August 14, 2026 promoting its Universal Merge Consensus work. Source: KIP-2 raw file.

vProgs have shipped as production app infrastructureRoadmap
What the repo shows instead

No. Zero releases, zero tags; the repo calls itself early development. Its tn10-flow example runs the flow against a private testnet-10 fork node, not the public one. A live Kaspa-native app layer needs its own shipped-product evidence. Source: vprogs repo.

Argent proves Kaspa smart contracts are production-readyResearch
What the README claims is present

No audit yet, and nothing has run on mainnet. Argent's own README says the main pieces are present: compiler, generated Silverscript, portable artifacts, runtime transaction building, multi-actor routing, and closed and open ICC. Silverscript has to finish its own audit first, then Argent needs hardening of its own. Source: Argent README.

Native DeFi has shipped as a mature Kaspa L1 product layerRoadmap
The covenant numbers behind that verdict

Genuine early usage at small scale, with lending still absent from mainnet. A third-party indexer counted 28,107 covenants ever created, 517 active, 27,590 burned, and about 1,750,093 KAS of live covenant value on August 22, across roughly 86 real markets. Kaskad/Igra-style L2 lending is ecosystem context around Kaspa, separate from native L1 DeFi. Source: app layer.
