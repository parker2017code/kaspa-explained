Kaspa Explained · Experiment

Discover

Not every job belongs on Kaspa L1, and rebuilding a working decentralized network from scratch is the wrong move when one already exists. This page is honest about that: it points to real, live, open-source projects for the parts of a "everything app" that a payments-and-messaging L1 chain does not itself provide.

← Back to the main wallet, messaging, feed, and rooms experiment

What Kaspa actually adds

Fast, cheap, non-custodial payments and small on-chain messages, proven earlier on this page: real TN10 transactions for wallets, encrypted DMs, group chat, a public feed, and a tip jar. That's a genuine, non-redundant contribution: a payment and identity rail other networks either lack or make slow and expensive. It is not a replacement for networks that already do music streaming, federated chat, or distributed storage well.

Music

Audius is a live, open-source, artist-owned music streaming protocol. Not built here, not affiliated with this experiment, linked because it already solves this job.

Audius ↗

Decentralized music streaming, artist-owned, open source, live today.

Chat, voice, communities

Matrix is a live, open, federated protocol for group chat, voice, and video, with no single company controlling the network. Element is its most widely used client.

Matrix ↗

Open federated chat and voice protocol. Anyone can run a homeserver.

 Element ↗

The main Matrix client: encrypted chat, voice, video, file and screen sharing.

Broader social

This experiment's own feed and rooms (on the main page) prove the pattern works on Kaspa L1 for a small, self-contained audience. Nostr is the live, open protocol for that same idea at internet scale: user-controlled identity, relay-based, no single company owns it.

Nostr ↗

Open social protocol. Users control their identity and choose their relays.

File storage

IPFS is the live, open content-addressed storage network. Filecoin adds a paid storage marketplace on top of it. Neither needs Kaspa to work, and Kaspa does not try to replace either.

IPFS ↗

Content-addressed, peer-to-peer file storage and distribution.

 Filecoin ↗

Decentralized storage marketplace built on IPFS.

Video

PeerTube runs peer-to-peer video directly in the browser across independently run instances. Livepeer is open video transcoding infrastructure for developers building on top.

PeerTube ↗

Federated, peer-to-peer video hosting, no central company.

 Livepeer ↗

Open video transcoding network for developers, not a viewer app itself.

What this page will not link to

No "everything downloader" and no unlicensed film or TV streaming tool. Decentralized hosting does not change copyright law, and that combination is the shape of a piracy tool regardless of the framing. If a legitimate, rights-cleared video use case shows up, it belongs in the Video section above using real infrastructure like PeerTube or Livepeer, not a link to bypass licensing.
