Checklist

How to analyze a coin

Token necessity, supply, launch design, security, validation, liquidity, market structure, and the strongest criticism against it come first. Check price last.

20-point checklist

Start with these questions

What problem does it solve? Why does the token need to exist, specifically? Could BTC, ETH, USDC, or a plain database do the same job? Who pays the fees, and for what? Who secures it: miners, validators, or a company? Who validates it, and how many independent parties can? Who can change the rules, and under what process? How did it launch: mined, sold, airdropped, or allocated? Who got early supply, and how much? Are there unlocks still coming, and when? What gives it durable demand beyond speculation? What pays for security once early incentives fade? What does it cost to run a node? What part of the system is actually centralized? What is private, and what is public by design? What would make the token unnecessary tomorrow? What is the specific failure mode? What would have to be true to justify the market cap? What is the strongest argument against it? Who profits if retail buys in right now?

Token necessity

Classify the token first

ClassificationWhat it meansCommon examples
Necessary native assetRemove the token and the network stops functioning: it pays fees, pays rewards, and secures the chain in one move.BTC, ETH, KAS, SOL in their own network contexts.
Helpful but optionalThe token coordinates incentives or access, but a different asset could plausibly fill the same role without breaking the design.Some app, infrastructure, and ecosystem tokens.
Governance-only tokenValue depends entirely on whether the governance vote controls real fees, parameters, or treasury funds, or just cosmetic settings.Many DeFi protocol tokens.
Claim or wrapperThe token represents something else held elsewhere. Its value rests on redemption rights, custody, bridge security, and contract trust rather than the token itself.Wrapped BTC, liquid staking tokens, tokenized gold or stocks.
Attention tokenCulture and tradable attention are the whole product. No underlying mechanism is claimed or required.Meme coins.
Likely unnecessaryAn existing asset or a normal database could run the same product with no loss of function.Many weak utility tokens.

Green and red flags

Fast risk scan

Green flags

  • A one-sentence answer for why the token has to exist.
  • Supply and launch history that anyone can check against on-chain data.
  • Active commits and documentation a stranger can read without an NDA.
  • Someone outside the founding team can run a validator or node and actually verify state.
  • Liquidity deep enough to exit a position that matters to you, with no unlock cliff dumping new supply at once.
  • Users who transact again after the initial airdrop claim.
  • The team names its own weak points before a critic does.

Red flags

  • Any promise of guaranteed yield or "no risk": yield without risk does not exist.
  • Insiders hold most of the supply while public float stays thin.
  • Partnerships that don't show up in the partner's own announcements.
  • No answer for why this needs a token instead of a database row.
  • Reserves, custody, bridges, or admin keys that nobody outside the team can inspect.
  • Influencer campaigns running well before there's a working product to campaign for.
  • Terms like "ecosystem," "unlock," or "infrastructure" doing the work a mechanism should do.

Market table

How to read a coin-ranking page

FieldWhat it meansWhat can mislead
PriceThe cost of one unit of the token.A $0.001 token is not "cheap." Divide by total supply before comparing it to anything.
Market capPrice multiplied by circulating supply.It measures none of the following: revenue, capital actually invested, or how much of that value could be sold without crashing the price.
VolumeAmount traded over a given period.Real liquidity, panic selling, hype buying, and wash trading all show up as the same number.
Circulating supplyTokens counted as available to trade."Circulating" does not mean "not controlled by insiders." Large blocks can sit with early holders and still count.
FDVPrice multiplied by total possible supply, including tokens not yet unlocked.A market cap that looks small next to a much larger FDV is a dilution warning.
Social dominanceShare of crypto social-media attention.Attention is not adoption, and it is not durable. It can vanish in a week.
Category labelsTags a market site assigns for search and filtering.Often too broad, stale, promotional, or wrong about the actual mechanism.

Kaspa lens

Apply it to Kaspa

KAS clears the token-necessity question more easily than most app tokens do: it is the asset a PoW UTXO blockDAG network tracks, charges fees in, and pays out as the mining reward. Remove KAS and there's no network left underneath it. The fair launch means no official insider allocation to discount for. That does not answer the rest of the checklist: liquidity, security budget, mining concentration, node requirements, adoption, funding, app-layer execution, and roadmap status are still open questions with their own evidence. The launch design doesn't settle them.
