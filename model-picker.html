How the score works, and where the numbers come from
  1. Three sources, and a model is listed only if all three scored it. LiveBench 2026-06-25 gives seven category scores and cost per successful task. Artificial Analysis gives an intelligence index, cost, output speed and time to first token. Agent Arena gives four session signals from 1.4 million real sessions. That rule is the whole relevance filter: it keeps the list to models people actually choose between, and it means every row is measured the same way.
  2. Each dial is a weight. Turn one up and it takes a bigger share. The shares shown next to the dials add to 100. Only open weights is a filter, because a model either ships its weights or it does not.
  3. A benchmark counts for what its evidence will bear, not for what it claims. Every dial is built from several figures, and the weight written against each one says how much it should matter for that job. That is an editorial call. Whether the figure can answer at all is not, so it is measured: how much of the field published it, and how far apart the top five actually are on it. Multiply the two and a thin or finished benchmark counts for a fraction of a well covered one that still separates the leaders. The effect is visible under each dial, and it is not always what the written weights suggest. Looking at images is 94 percent MMMU Pro and 6 percent Arena Vision, because MMMU Pro has fifteen of the 23 models against Arena Vision's four. Answering fast is two thirds output speed, because time to first token has a top-five spread of 0.3 points and cannot rank anything. The same two numbers are computed for all 39 figures. Nothing is tuned per benchmark.
  4. Scores are percentiles across the models that published that number, not the raw benchmark values, so a 90 means near the top of this field rather than 90%.
  5. One row per model, at its best published setting. The leaderboards disagree about which reasoning effort to publish: LiveBench scored Claude Fable 5 at max effort, Artificial Analysis scored its default. Nothing is ever copied from one setting to another; the row simply carries what each source actually measured.
  6. No leaderboard composite is imported, and that now includes the two that were. Arena's Net Improvement is the number its own rank is built from, so copying it would replay their ranking instead of building one. LiveBench's overall and Artificial Analysis's intelligence index are composites too, and both used to feed the score while their own sub-scores were also counted here, so a model strong at reasoning earned it twice. Both are gone. Every factor is built from individual published figures. Arena's Tool Hallucination column stays out for a different reason: one value fills fifteen of 44 rows and all ten places of its own top-ten list, which is a placeholder rather than a measurement.
  7. Sixteen factors, and all three leaderboards feed them. Artificial Analysis publishes sixteen individual benchmarks next to its index, and the index was the only thing read here until 1 August 2026. All sixteen are now in, including the eight added on 2 August: Terminal-Bench v2.1, tau-2 Telecom and tau-3 Banking tool use, Omniscience knowledge, GPQA Diamond, SciCode, IFBench and APEX agentic office work, plus MMMU Pro visual reasoning. Thirty-seven of the thirty-nine figures loaded now feed the score. The two that do not are the two composites.
  8. Not every model is measured on every factor, and the gap runs one way. A model is scored across the figures it published, which quietly rewards not publishing: a model with no vision score skips vision, while one that posted a weak vision score carries it. That is how a partly measured model can outrank a fully measured one. Two guards, both visible. A model needs published figures covering 70 percent of the weight you set or it is not ranked at all, so nothing is ever scored on a fraction of the question. And any row not measured on everything you asked for says which factors it missed and what share of your question its score actually covers. Each dial also prints how much of the field its figures cover, because a thin benchmark and a saturated one fail differently and only the second was labeled before.
  9. Arena's four boards are thin, so vision does not rest on one. Arena Vision and Arena Document carry four of the 23 models each and Arena Text three, and none of the three has a single open-weights entry. Asking for vision with open weights on therefore used to return an empty list with no explanation. Vision now leads on Artificial Analysis's MMMU Pro, which covers fifteen models including three open-weights ones, with Arena Vision behind it at lower weight. Reading documents still rests on Arena Document alone, so it stays thin and says so, and the empty state now names whichever factor emptied the list instead of guessing.
  10. Most benchmarks are saturated, and each factor says whether it can separate the leaders. Across the top five models GPQA Diamond spans a single point, long-context reasoning one, Terminal-Bench v2.1 four, SciCode four, time to first token zero. Those cannot rank a frontier that has already maxed them out, and a score leaning on them is ranking noise with a decimal point on it. Each dial carries its own frontier spread, computed from the loaded data, and reads separates, barely separates, or cannot separate. Ask for something in that last group and the page says the order is inside measurement noise rather than letting a one-point gap read as a verdict.
  11. Hallucination is the widest gap at the frontier, so it is its own factor. Eighteen points of spread across the top five, wider than any coding, reasoning or agentic figure here. This is Artificial Analysis's measured non-hallucination rate, not Arena's Tool Hallucination column, which stays out because one value fills fifteen of its 44 rows.
  12. Arena prints its signals with the minus signs stripped. Read as printed, the worst-ranked model looks like the best at recovering from a failed command. Each signal's own top-ten list gives a threshold that recovers the sign, and that is done in data/arena-agent.json before anything is scored.
  13. Cost is per finished task, not per token. A cheap model that needs three attempts is not cheap.
