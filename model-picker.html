Effort-tier gaps

How a missing tier gets filled

A few rows here needed a figure their source never published at that exact effort tier, most often cost per successful task. Six model families on this board have two or more measured effort settings, real tier-to-tier steps this page learns from: cost drops about a third a step, response time drops faster than that, and accuracy figures fall 1 to 17 percent depending on which one, never a straight line.

Each missing tier is anchored off whichever real row sits closest to it and filled automatically, silently, never mixed into a real measured row without being marked. Estimates going down the ladder read the curve straight; estimates going up invert it, a shakier guess since a low-effort score can sit on a floor that will not un-flatten cleanly. Every estimated row says which direction it came from.

Ranking

How the score works, and where the numbers come from
  1. Three sources, and every dial is a blend rather than a single figure. Artificial Analysis, LiveBench 2026-06-25, and LM Arena, all read 14 August 2026. Twenty models, one row per effort setting actually published: Claude Opus 5 is two rows here, not one, because high effort and max effort are different published configurations with different cost and speed.
  2. Each of the ten dials blends two or three benchmarks that measure the same underlying thing. Reasoning blends LiveBench reasoning with Humanity's Last Exam and GPQA Diamond. Agentic work blends LM Arena Agent with Terminal-Bench Hard and Terminal-Bench v2.1. No dial rests on one number a single lab happened to publish.
  3. Missing one of a dial's benchmarks does not zero the dial. A model scored on two of a dial's three benchmarks is blended from those two, silently, with no per-model caveat cluttering the row. A dial only reads no data for a model when every benchmark behind it is absent for that model.
  4. Three rows here carry no Artificial Analysis data at all: Claude Opus 4.8 (High), Claude Opus 4.7 (High), and GPT-5.5 (xHigh). That is a real, disclosed coverage gap, not a bug. Every Artificial-Analysis-only dial reads no data for those three; LiveBench- and Arena-backed dials still score them on whatever those two sources published.
  5. No leaderboard's own headline score is used. Artificial Analysis's intelligence index, LiveBench's own composite, and Arena's own Elo rank all feed nothing here. The individual figures go in instead, and the dial weights below decide how they combine.
  6. Each dial is a weight. Turn one up and it takes a bigger share of the score. The shares shown next to the dials add to 100. Only open weights is a filter, because a model either ships its weights or it does not.
  7. A benchmark counts for what its evidence will bear, not for what it claims. The weight written against each figure says how much it should matter, which is an editorial call. Whether the figure can answer at all is measured instead: how much of this roster published it, and how far apart the top five models actually are on it. A thin or saturated benchmark counts for a fraction of a well covered one that still separates the leaders.
  8. Scores are percentiles across the twenty models here, not raw benchmark values, so a 90 means near the top of this specific field rather than 90 percent correct.
  9. Effort-tier gaps are filled automatically, from the same six-family curve documented under each dial's provenance, never mixed silently into a measured row. Three cost figures on this board are filled this way because LiveBench published only one nearby effort tier for that model family: Claude Opus 5 (High), Claude Opus 4.8 (High), and Claude Opus 4.7 (High) each show an estimated cost per successful task, anchored off the nearest tier LiveBench actually measured.
  10. Not every model is measured on every factor, and the gap runs one way. A model is scored across the figures it published, which quietly rewards not publishing. A model with at least one published figure among what you asked for is ranked on that; one with none of them is not ranked at all. Any row not measured on everything you asked for says which factors it missed and what share of your question its score actually covers.
  11. Most benchmarks are saturated at the frontier, and each dial says whether it can separate the leaders. A dial ranking a frontier that has already maxed out its benchmarks is ranking noise with a decimal point on it. Each dial carries its own frontier spread, computed from the loaded data, and reads separates, barely separates, or cannot separate.
  12. Cost leads with the finished task, then blends in two raw price signals. The cheap dial's biggest share is LiveBench's cost per successful task, because a cheap model that needs three attempts is not cheap. Artificial Analysis's cost per task and output price per 1M tokens fill the rest of the dial at lower weight, a second independent cost source and the model's raw sticker price.
