How the score works, and where the numbers come from
  1. Three sources, and a model is listed only if all three scored it. LiveBench 2026-06-25 gives seven category scores and cost per successful task. Artificial Analysis gives its individual benchmarks, named below. Arena gives four session signals from 1.4 million real sessions. That rule is the whole relevance filter: it keeps the list to models people actually choose between, and it means every row is measured the same way.
  2. No leaderboard's own headline score is used. Artificial Analysis publishes an intelligence index and it feeds nothing here. It is a weighted blend of the same benchmarks listed below, so importing it alongside them would count each one twice, at a mix somebody else chose. The individual figures go in instead and the factors below decide the weights. Every one of the twenty is named on the factor it drives, so a dial reading forty-three percent Terminal-Bench Hard tells you which measurement moved it. The same applies to LiveBench's overall column.
  3. The benchmarks were picked for spread at the frontier, not for prestige. A benchmark the leaders have all maxed out cannot rank them, whatever its reputation. Hard reasoning goes to Humanity's Last Exam and CritPt physics, where the frontier has not saturated, while GPQA Diamond enters at low weight because across the top five it spans a single point. Terminal-Bench v2.1 carries the terminal factor, because Terminal-Bench Hard was published by too few of the field to rank it. Each dial prints its own frontier spread, computed from the loaded figures, and says whether it separates, barely separates, or cannot separate the leaders.
  4. Each dial is a weight. Turn one up and it takes a bigger share. The shares shown next to the dials add to 100. Only open weights is a filter, because a model either ships its weights or it does not.
  5. A benchmark counts for what its evidence will bear, not for what it claims. Every dial is built from several figures, and the weight written against each one says how much it should matter for that job. That is an editorial call. Whether the figure can answer at all is not, so it is measured: how much of the field published it, and how far apart the top five actually are on it. Multiply the two and a thin or finished benchmark counts for a fraction of a well covered one that still separates the leaders. The effect is visible under each dial, and it is not always what the written weights suggest. Answering fast is two thirds output speed, because time to first token has a top-five spread of 0.3 points and cannot rank anything. The same two numbers are computed for all 29 figures. Nothing is tuned per benchmark.
  6. Scores are percentiles across the models that published that number, not the raw benchmark values, so a 90 means near the top of this field rather than 90%.
  7. One row per model, at its best published setting. The leaderboards disagree about which reasoning effort to publish: LiveBench scored Claude Fable 5 at max effort, Artificial Analysis scored its default. Nothing is ever copied from one setting to another; the row simply carries what each source actually measured.
  8. No leaderboard composite is imported, and that now includes the two that were. Arena's Net Improvement is the number its own rank is built from, so copying it would replay their ranking instead of building one. LiveBench's overall and Artificial Analysis's intelligence index are composites too, and both used to feed the score while their own sub-scores were also counted here, so a model strong at reasoning earned it twice. Both are gone. Every factor is built from individual published figures. Arena's Tool Hallucination column stays out for a different reason: one value fills fifteen of 44 rows and all ten places of its own top-ten list, which is a placeholder rather than a measurement.
  9. A figure has to be measured on most of the field to count. A benchmark only a handful of models published cannot rank the rest: everyone who skipped it scores zero, the 70 percent coverage rule then drops them from any ranking that asks for it, and the dial quietly ranks whoever happened to publish rather than whoever is best. So a figure is loaded only if at least 80 percent of the field reported it, and a model is listed only if it reported at least 90 percent of those figures. That cut ten figures and three models on 7 August 2026, and what is left is 29 figures across 20 models with almost no gaps.
  10. Not every model is measured on every factor, and the gap runs one way. A model is scored across the figures it published, which quietly rewards not publishing: a model with no vision score skips vision, while one that posted a weak vision score carries it. That is how a partly measured model can outrank a fully measured one. Two guards, both visible. A model needs published figures covering 70 percent of the weight you set or it is not ranked at all, so nothing is ever scored on a fraction of the question. And any row not measured on everything you asked for says which factors it missed and what share of your question its score actually covers. Each dial also prints how much of the field its figures cover, because a thin benchmark and a saturated one fail differently and only the second was labeled before.
  11. Arena's four head-to-head boards are gone, and so are the dials that rested on them. Only the top fifteen of each board publishes a score at all, so the figure was structurally biased toward models already winning, and it covered between four and ten of the field. Building an interface, Reading documents, Looking at images and Debugging live systems were built on those boards and on two other thin benchmarks, and all four dials came out with them rather than being left to rank a handful of models behind a general-sounding label. Arena's four session signals stay, because they cover every listed model.
  12. Most benchmarks are saturated, and each factor says whether it can separate the leaders. Across the top five models GPQA Diamond spans a single point, long-context reasoning one, Terminal-Bench v2.1 four, SciCode four, time to first token zero. Those cannot rank a frontier that has already maxed them out, and a score leaning on them is ranking noise with a decimal point on it. Each dial carries its own frontier spread, computed from the loaded data, and reads separates, barely separates, or cannot separate. Ask for something in that last group and the page says the order is inside measurement noise rather than letting a one-point gap read as a verdict.
  13. Hallucination is the widest gap at the frontier, so it is its own factor. Eighteen points of spread across the top five, wider than any coding, reasoning or agentic figure here. This is Artificial Analysis's measured non-hallucination rate, not Arena's Tool Hallucination column, which stays out because one value fills fifteen of its 44 rows.
  14. Arena prints its signals with the minus signs stripped. Read as printed, the worst-ranked model looks like the best at recovering from a failed command. Each signal's own top-ten list gives a threshold that recovers the sign, and that is done in data/arena-agent.json before anything is scored.
  15. Cost is per finished task, not per token. A cheap model that needs three attempts is not cheap.
