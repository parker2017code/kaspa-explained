Kaspa vs Ethereum for apps

If your app needs deployed smart contracts, DeFi liquidity, or audited tooling today, Ethereum has them and Kaspa does not. What Kaspa has is live PoW payments plus Toccata's activated covenant rules, a smaller surface that fits a narrower kind of product.

Status: payments are live today. Toccata activated at DAA score 474,165,565 on Rusty Kaspa v2.0.1. Wallet support, tooling, and app receipts for the new rule surface are still catching up.

Kaspa vs Ethereum by app maturity

Build on Ethereum or its L2s when your product needs deployed smart contracts, composable DeFi, stablecoin liquidity, audited toolchains, existing wallets, or mature indexers today. Those categories exist on Ethereum because years of deployed contracts, audits, and integrations accumulated on top of each other. Kaspa does not have a live answer to any of those yet.

Where Kaspa is worth building around

Fast PoW payment feel

Wallets and receipts can build on the live network now, no roadmap dependency.

UTXO rule design

Vault, escrow, asset, and funding rules map onto constrained outputs now that Toccata is activated.

Shared sequencing thesis

Based apps and later vProgs share Kaspa's ordering and proof surfaces instead of running on separate execution.

Status labels

Live, testnet, targeted, roadmap, or research: every claim here carries one, so you know which parts you can build on today.

A category name is not a product

Calling something "Kaspa lending" or "Kaspa DeFi" borrows a category from Ethereum without doing the work the name implies. On Ethereum, "lending" means specific audited contracts, oracle feeds, and liquidation logic that have run through years of adversarial testing. A Kaspa version of lending, markets, launch tools, or stable assets needs its own custody model, oracle assumptions, liquidity, wallet path, and evidence that the behavior it claims is actually accepted on-chain.
