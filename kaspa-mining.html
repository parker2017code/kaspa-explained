The two markets

A proof-of-work coin runs two markets on two clocks: one reprices in minutes, the other answers months later.

Coin market minutes to days

price, liquidity, sentiment

Machine market weeks to months

ASIC orders, shipping, hosting, hash rate, difficulty

Hash rate can fall and still sit far above last year's level, while used ASIC prices collapse into demand that already cooled.

Cycle model

Coins and ASICs heat and cool at different times

This framing is an outside opinion, not a sourced protocol fact: an X account, @Themooseisloos5, cast the coin-and-ASIC cycle as a four-phase Carnot-engine model.

Four phases

The hot/cold cycle and Kaspa's compressed history

Phase-by-phase breakdown of coin and ASIC market timing.
1

Hot coins, cold ASICs

Demand moves in before hardware can respond, so margins expand.

2

Hot coins, hot ASICs

New machines get ordered and hash rate climbs.

3

Cold coins, hot ASICs

Price cools while ordered machines keep arriving, crushing margins.

4

Cold coins, cold ASICs

Weak miners shut off, and the system compresses until it finds balance.

Kaspa compressed these phases into one short window instead of Bitcoin's years, which cuts both ways: bigger swings up and down.

Five signals, the too-fast take, and the slower reading

Every row below is a chart people read wrong in public, most weeks.

Signal Too-fast reading Slower reading
Price downThe market lost interest.Lost demand, miner selling, early-holder profit-taking, broad market weakness, and leverage unwinds can all produce the same line, separately or together.
Hash rate downThe network is failing.Weaker security is one explanation. Inefficient miners leaving after an overheated ASIC cycle is another. Compare against long-term history and attack cost before picking one.

Fees riseHigher fees are automatically good.Fees from repeat use people value help miners. Spam and one-off bursts push the number up without meaning the same thing.

Checklist

What healthier, worse, and genuinely unknown look like

Signals across hash rate, distribution, tooling, and fees.

Healthier

  • Hash rate stabilizes alongside better operator distribution.
  • Wallets, explorers, SDKs, and solo-mining paths keep improving.
  • Toccata generates repeat app use and fees miners collect.

Worse

  • Hash rate keeps falling alongside worse mining concentration.
  • Price and volume keep weakening with no demand replacing the hype that left.
  • Fees stay irrelevant because apps don't retain users.

Nobody knows live

  • How much selling came from miners versus holders versus broader flows.
  • Whether future fees can replace enough issuance as the reward declines.
  • Whether the next major cycle runs up, down, or sideways.

Price alone is too narrow a signal, and so is hash rate alone; read them together.
