## Standing instructions from the owner, 22 August

**Agents adapt on the fly.** Research will surface things nobody scoped for.
An agent that finds a better question than the one it was handed should chase
it and say so, rather than completing a stale brief precisely. Every agent
brief carries that permission. What it does not carry is permission to invent:
an unverified finding gets reported as unverified, never smoothed into the
main list.

**The banned-word lint was cut.** The owner called it nonsense and he is right.
It blocked two commits in one day over words that were carrying meaning rather
than padding. A word list cannot tell padding from emphasis. What stays in the
gate is what a machine can actually judge: em dashes, reading grade, repeated
devices, claim freshness, link integrity, HTML validity. Taste is not on that
list and should not be enforced by grep.

Judgment is the standard, not compliance. If a rule in this repo is wrong, fix
the rule and say so in the commit.

## How decisions get made when research changes the picture

Written because the owner asked, and because five agents finding things in
parallel will otherwise each answer this differently.

**The ranking test.** Would a reader who already follows Kaspa closely learn
something, or argue with it? Announcements usually fail. A named contributor
arguing against a design most people assume is settled usually passes.
Reversals, disagreements and quietly-false claims outrank new features.

**Five states, never blurred.** A number used in conversation. An open
proposal. A merged draft. An accepted standard. An activated protocol rule.
Most of the site's errors are one of these wearing another's clothes. Every
correction has to say which state a thing is actually in.

**Both directions of error.** Overstatement is the obvious one. Understatement
is as wrong and much harder to catch, because a live feature described as
pending never sounds like hype. Hunt both.

**When sources disagree**, the release tag and the on-chain activation record
win over docs pages and prose. kaspa.org is currently describing Toccata as
testnet-only roughly eight weeks after mainnet activation, which is the proof
that this rule is needed rather than theoretical.

**Verified and unverified never mix.** An unverified finding is reported as
unverified, in its own list. A claim inside an open PR is a claim inside an
open PR, not a fact, no matter how confident it sounds.

**Scope changes are allowed and must be declared.** An agent that finds a
better question than the one it was handed should chase it and say what it
dropped. Silent scope drift is the failure, not scope change.

## Definition of done

Stop when all of these hold. Not before, and importantly, not after.

1. CLAIMS.yml has no stale recheck_after date, and every date that moved was
   moved because the claim behind it was rechecked against a primary source.
2. No page contradicts the verified protocol state, in either direction.
3. 25 posts exist for 22 August through 15 September, each resting on a
   verified fact, each citing its source.
4. A red team has recomputed the numbers and read the run, and its findings are
   either fixed or explicitly recorded as accepted.
5. `bash scripts/check-site.sh` prints "Site checks passed." on a CLEAN CLONE
   of the pushed commit.
6. The live site serves the changes, confirmed with a cache-busting fetch.

Explicitly NOT in scope, so this does not loop: rewriting pages that are merely
old but still true, redesigning anything that works, or a new page that does
not answer a question no current page answers. New pages are opt-in and have to
argue for themselves first.

## Known traps carried forward

- Never bump a `recheck_after` date without rechecking the claim itself.
- Numbers computed after a pipeline change go stale silently. Six instances of
  that bug class are already recorded in WORKING-STATE.md.
- A repo-created date is not a ship date. The kccs repo existing does not mean
  any KCC is adopted.
- Do not flatten Toccata layers into "smart contracts are live".
