App concept

Coordination markets

A coordination market only pays out once enough strangers commit to the same condition. Kaspa's fast mined ordering gives the design a shared, checkable record, and Toccata's covenant rules can enforce a threshold release on L1.

Status: the L1 pieces are live; the market is not. No wallet flow, tooling, or app receipts exist yet for a working coordination market.

How a coordination market would work

A user commits funds. The app groups that commitment with others aiming at the same condition. A public rule checks whether the condition is met. Funds release or refund based on that check, visible to anyone replaying the chain.

A website can run that same loop, and usually a website is the right answer. The version worth putting on a chain is the one where participants expect the operator to move the threshold after seeing the pledges, reorder who got in first, quietly refund a friend, or stop answering email. A covenant moves the release condition out of the operator's database and into the spend rule, where breaking it means producing a transaction consensus rejects.

Speed matters here for a specific reason. At 10 blocks per second a commitment reaches the shared record without an auction waiting on the block interval. Fast inclusion is not final settlement, though, so a market that pays out on the first commitment through the door has to decide which confirmation depth it treats as settled and say so before anyone signs.

All of that is buildable with public, replayable logic. Privacy before the threshold is hit, reusable capital, solver incentives, and oracle markets remain unsolved design problems.

Where it could fit

Assurance funding

Software, research, events, local infrastructure, or public goods that only get funded once a pledge threshold is reached, under rules fixed before anyone commits.

Hackathon bounties

A prize releases when objective evidence or judge approval satisfies a rule that was published before the work started.

Resource auctions

Compute, data, access, or service capacity allocated through commitments anyone can check, unlike a sealed order book.

Oracle-backed markets

Prediction, insurance, and payment markets that turn on external data. The oracle's trust model, incentives, and dispute path have to be stated up front, because the covenant cannot check them.

First-to-know signal markets

Miners or other rewarded reporters attest to an event, and the market surfaces the earliest credible signal before normal feeds catch up.

AI-agent tasks

Deposits, machine-checkable completion proofs, release and refund paths, and evidence an agent can point at during a dispute with another agent.

Start from one narrow market

Pick one condition, one payout rule, and a group small enough to name every participant. Assurance funding for a single recurring event is a real test with real users at the end of it. A general solver nobody has used is not.
